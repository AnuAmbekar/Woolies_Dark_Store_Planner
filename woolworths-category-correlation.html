Category Spend Correlation Matrix

Which categories tend to be bought together, based on customer-level spend

How to read this

Each customer's total spend was added up for every product category. The matrix compares every pair of categories across all customers and asks: when a customer spends more in one category, do they also tend to spend more in the other? A score near +1.00 means the two categories rise and fall together — a strong sign shoppers buy them on the same trip. A score near 0.00 means the two spend patterns are unrelated. A score near −1.00 means one going up tends to come with the other going down.

Categories are ordered along both axes in the store's business sequence (Milk → Bread → Eggs → Pasta → Pasta Sauce → Nachos → Salsa → Diapers → Wet wipes → Chocolates), not alphabetically, so neighbouring rows/columns reflect how the range is actually organised.

Method & assumptions

  • Spend per line = quantity × unit price (matches the dataset's total_price column, verified to reconcile on every row).
  • Per-customer, per-category spend was summed across all their orders, giving a 100 customer × 10 category matrix, then Pearson correlation was computed column-to-column on that matrix.
  • Customers with zero spend in a category are included as 0, not excluded — a customer who never buys diapers is genuine signal, not a missing value.
  • Category order follows the letter prefixes already present in the source data (A.Milk … J.Chocolates), read as the intended business/planogram sequence rather than re-sorted alphabetically by name.
